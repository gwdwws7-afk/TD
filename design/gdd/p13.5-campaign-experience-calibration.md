# P13.5 Campaign Experience Calibration

## Goal

P13.5 validates the entire campaign as played combat rather than as authored numbers.
It must show that difficulty, formations, upgrade branches, build sites, routes, map
mechanics, economy, and Boss phases produce distinct and explainable decisions across
all 20 levels.

The release target is not universal victory. Standard must remain consistently
finishable with several plans, while Veteran and Ember Trial must separate through
pace, armor, reserve pressure, and command cost. Difficulty must not be manufactured
by adding enemy health solely to consume excess money.

## Runtime Matrix

The release matrix contains 190 accelerated rendered Unity sessions:

- 180 campaign runs: 20 levels, three difficulties, and three strategy profiles.
- 10 mechanic counterfactuals: engage and hold policies on L05, L09, L13, L17, and
  L20.
- Every run uses real spawning, movement, targeting, projectiles, upgrades, scenario
  commands, Boss transitions, result calculation, UI layout audits, and Console
  checks.
- The wrapper explicitly applies the scheduled difficulty before every deployment and
  validates level, difficulty, strategy, and mechanic policy before reusing a report.
  A player's saved per-level preference cannot silently relabel a matrix sample.
- Acceleration changes wall-clock duration only. Wave order, game time, combat
  updates, and result telemetry remain runtime behavior.

The three strategy profiles are Focused Fire, Control Lattice, and Adaptive Network.
They are deterministic for a fixed seed but intentionally differ in formation,
placement, branch, command, and route-response decisions.

## Decision Telemetry

Each run records:

- Per-wave budget start/end, income, purchases, readiness, integrity, escapes, and
  failure signals.
- Per-lane spawned health, damage, escapes, and normalized route entropy.
- Per-tower kind, cell, build wave, first/last upgrade wave, damage and utility branch
  counts, specialization, spend, damage, kills, and control applications.
- First-wave pressure, final-five-wave spend conversion, ending reserve, top site,
  top tower kind, and three precise replay recommendations.
- Scenario mechanic policy and activation waves, builds and upgrades made after the
  first activation, and Boss phases triggered or suppressed.
- Placement, composition, branch, and combined replay signatures.

## Calibration Rules

- First-wave pressure must remain below 85. Standard may leak at most four enemies in
  wave one, Veteran six, and Ember Trial eight.
- From L05 onward, a site may contribute at most 58% of run damage.
- From L05 onward, when more than one tower kind is available and used, one kind may
  contribute at most 78% of combined value. Combined value uses the existing
  `damage + control applications x 8` analytics rule; raw damage share is exported
  separately.
- At least 90% of victories must retain meaningful final-five-wave spending
  decisions, and no victory may end above 999 reserve.
- At least 95% of comparable strategy groups must produce two or more distinct replay
  signatures.
- Standard focus-level losses may not exceed 15% of their scheduled samples.
- L20 victories using an active Boss policy must observe at least two Boss phases.
- Standard, Veteran, and Ember Trial must remain ordered by win rate and median
  escapes, with a measurable separation in victory, leakage, or first-wave pressure.

The earlier L07/L15 32% site threshold remains the Standard target. Runtime audits use
35% for Veteran and 38% for Ember Trial because faster enemies shift contribution
toward earlier interception sites even when the same authored layout remains viable.
L01-L04 still export site and tower-kind shares, but their limited tutorial roster and
low enemy health make the first unlocked answer dominate damage by construction; they
are excluded from the full-roster dominance hard gate.

## Map Mechanic Counterfactuals

The five chapter mechanics are accepted only when engaging them changes at least one
real defense decision relative to holding it:

| Level | Mechanic | Expected decision surface |
|---:|---|---|
| L05 | Timed reinforcement | Reserve timing, reinforcement response, branch timing |
| L09 | Route switch | Route coverage, placement depth, control composition |
| L13 | Environment device | Command spend, damage window, specialization timing |
| L17 | Boss phase control | Phase suppression, anti-heavy composition, reserve |
| L20 | Boss phase control | Multi-phase pacing, command timing, late upgrade order |

The comparison uses placement, composition, and branch signatures. A score-only or
enemy-stat-only delta does not qualify as a changed decision.

## P13.5 Corrections

- Veteran and Ember Trial now separate through speed, armor, starting reserve, reward
  rate, line integrity, and scenario command cost while preserving their existing
  health multipliers. The final Ember Trial profile uses 1.15 speed, +3 armor, -28
  starting budget, -4 integrity, and 1.30 scenario cost. The final rendered matrix
  keeps a measurable difficulty gap without adding more enemy health.
- Resonance-chain cash bonuses were reduced so late commands support a decision
  instead of refilling a saturated economy.
- Control Lattice may expand after waves 12 and 16, converting late reserve into
  route coverage rather than stopping at an early soft cap.
- Focused Fire now uses Rail, Siege, Flak, and Beacon as its primary identity. It is a
  break-and-finish chain rather than a disguised Cinder Mortar stack.
- Upgrade selection lowers the priority of a site after its contribution share rises
  above 28%, avoids investing early in towers with no observed contribution, and
  stops tier-two sites above 26% contribution from compounding again from L07 onward.
- Default placement uses a mild first-30% output penalty through L06. From L07 onward,
  output towers are excluded from the first four grid columns while the explicit
  entrance-site A/B variant remains available. This keeps the tutorial chapters open
  without restoring L07's dominant entrance kill zone.
- Cinder Mortar keeps its five-target splash identity. Edge splash is 38% and base
  impact is 16, leaving the tower as the swarm answer without allowing one central
  mortar to determine a full run.
- Arc Welder keeps three-target chaining and its utility range identity, but base
  impact is 10. This prevents one chain tower on a crossing from exhausting the wave
  before the rest of the formation can contribute.
- Hollow Kiln's authored sites clustered around the L15 multi-route convergence and
  exceeded the 32% runtime target with different tower kinds even after one central
  site moved. The complete twelve-site set is redistributed across columns 0 through
  15. Every site keeps at least 0.69-cell road clearance and two-cell Manhattan
  spacing, so each position serves a branch or route depth instead of the same merge.
- Hollow Kiln output towers inside the first 30% of route depth stop after one
  autoplay upgrade once the formation has four towers. This preserves their opening
  interception role without letting the dual-route cell at 4,6 become the entire
  damage plan. L15 Standard Adaptive retained a zero-leak clear, 181 ending budget,
  six final-five-wave purchases, and reduced its maximum site share from 35.7% to
  26.4%.
- Hollow Kiln limits Resonance Beacon repetition to one in Focused Fire and two in
  the other strategies. The L13 Focused result kept a zero-leak clear and reduced
  tower-kind contribution from 90.3% to 44.8%, while retaining two purchases in the
  final five waves.
- Hollow Kiln Focused Fire anchors its one Beacon at cell 4,6 so the support slot
  contributes at route depth instead of leaving the tenth build unused. Its two
  persistent-UI-obscured sites moved to 13,3 and 11,7.
- Grayline's marginal cell 8,1 moved 0.10 world units inward. The final geometry
  audit retains all twelve useful authored sites without accepting a road-overlap
  tolerance exception.
- Last Ember focused output towers use the same absolute entrance exclusion for all
  site variants, and late upgrades/builds convert final-five-wave reserve instead of
  stopping at the earlier soft cap.
- L19 wave 20 clear reward is 76 rather than 78. This local economy correction moved
  the Ember Focused ending reserve from 1000 to 979 while preserving four late
  purchases, avoiding a global income or enemy-health adjustment.
- Scenario-aware engage formations balance among the mechanic's preferred tower
  kinds instead of repeating the first preferred kind.

The fixed-seed cross-regression after these corrections kept L05 at zero opening and
total escapes. L07 Standard also cleared with all three strategies at 17.1%, 30.3%,
and 22.3% maximum site damage share for Focused Fire, Control Lattice, and Adaptive
Network respectively; all retained meaningful late-wave economy decisions.

## Automation

Run the full release matrix:

```powershell
./tools/td_mcp_p135_campaign_experience_calibration.ps1 `
  -Scope Release `
  -TimeScale 32 `
  -MaxRealSeconds 110 `
  -OutputRoot output/playtest/p135_release_v1
```

The wrapper can resume completed runs with `-ResumeExisting`. Individual run keys can
be forced with `-RerunKeys`.

The deterministic 180-run fast matrix remains a forecasting layer:

```powershell
./tools/td_mcp_p102_balance_matrix.ps1 `
  -OutputDirectory output/playtest/p135_fast_final
```

Fast-model warnings are not accepted or rejected without rendered runtime evidence.

## Final Release Evidence

The final rendered matrix completed all 190 sessions and passed every release gate:

- 180/180 main samples completed and 179/180 won. Standard and Veteran are 60/60;
  Ember Trial is 59/60 with 0.78 average escapes versus 0 and 0.10.
- The sole loss is L13 Ember Trial Control Lattice at wave 18. All twelve leaks are
  attributed to `counter_mismatch`; armor-counter damage is 0%, and the review points
  to the left route and recommends Rail Lancer or Siege Drill. The other two L13
  strategies clear without leaks.
- First-wave, site dominance, tower-kind dominance, late economy, replay difference,
  map mechanic, focus-level, Boss phase, telemetry, surface, and difficulty-order
  gates all pass.
- Meaningful final-five-wave economy decisions remain in 98.3% of victories and no
  victory ends above 999 reserve.
- All five mechanic A/B pairs change real placement or upgrade-branch decisions.
  L09, L13, L17, and L20 also change composition.
- All seven focus levels satisfy the release gates. L07 Ember stays at 0 opening and
  total escapes with a maximum 35.1% site share; L15 Ember remains 3/3 with a maximum
  28.7% site share; L20 Ember is 3/3 with 9-10 observed Boss phases.

Independent final regressions also pass:

- P13.1 L07/L15 opening and dominance matrix: 18/18.
- P12.0 geometry audit: 6/6 probes across five maps.
- P13.3 combat readability matrix: 8/8 across 1280 and 960 surfaces.
- P10.2 deterministic forecast: 180/180, stable repeat fingerprint, no stalls, no
  Console issues, and all five milestone exams passed.

## Residual Risks

- The deterministic fast model remains pessimistic relative to rendered combat. It
  reports five curve warnings, including an L14-L16 Ember zero-win streak, while the
  rendered matrix clears all nine corresponding samples. Keep it as an early warning
  layer and recalibrate its combat abstraction before using its win rates as targets.
- Siege Drill appears in 20% of runs and has 44.6% zero-contribution towers. Grav
  Snare appears in 8.3% with 31.1% zero contribution. Their specialist identity is
  visible, but future roster work should improve placement/value prediction rather
  than buffing raw damage globally.
- Rail Lancer is appropriately common at 85% usage, but 30.4% of built copies record
  zero contribution. Late placement scoring should remain under observation in
  player telemetry even though site and kind dominance both pass.

## Release Boundary

P13.5 may tune authored pacing, economy multipliers, tower composition, upgrade
selection, and site policy. It does not add a new tower, enemy family, progression
currency, or map. Remaining failures must be reported as a concrete next calibration
target rather than hidden by a broader system.
